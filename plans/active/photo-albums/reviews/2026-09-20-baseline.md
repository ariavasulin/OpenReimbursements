# Ready for review

DWS Photos already has a clean, photo-led foundation. The largest gaps are readability on phones, a clear answer to “where are my folders?”, and action screens that sound like internal administration tools. Keep the dark palette and the grid; make orientation and everyday actions much more explicit.

## Verified premise and review scope

The brief establishes non-technical design-build staff uploading from phones and browsing from office desktops, with an older, folder-minded owner coming from Mylio. The moment is a working photo library and its import/recovery flows. The key questions are “where are my photos?”, “is this the right job/photo?”, and “can I safely leave or undo this?”. Browsing a job and opening a photo is the focal action for this audit; field capture/upload is the complementary phone workflow. Visit frequency was not quantified in the brief.

This is an audit of the live app as supplied, before the planned navigation redesign. Recommendations below are audit judgments against that audience, not a newly approved product design.

**Browser session:** Microsoft Playwright CLI, session `peprkit-delegate-fe20-worker`, against `https://photos.design-workshops.app`. All findings below are **executed** observations of that surface, supported by saved screenshots and, where stated, live DOM measurements. Association of this deployment with any worktree is **author-vouched**, not independently verified.

Required CLI preflight passed: Vercel returned a logged-in user; Supabase listed Receipt App. SMS sign-in succeeded on the first attempt; the code arrived. Only the newest permitted sign-in message was read. Repository source was neither inspected nor changed; mandatory repository guidance was read.

## Looks

Findings are ordered by user impact within this list.

### L1 — High: enlarged text pushes the phone Upload action offscreen

**Where / evidence:** Project grid, 390×844 with Chromium’s default text size doubled from 16px to 32px: [project-browser-text-200-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/project-browser-text-200-phone.png). Desktop comparison, 1440×1000: [project-browser-text-200-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/project-browser-text-200-desktop.png).

**Observed:** On phone, the fixed bottom action row no longer fits: “Upload” extends beyond the right edge. On desktop, surrounding controls enlarge while the project heading and job names remain small. Live computed styles confirmed a 32px root/body but a 17px project heading.

**Why it matters:** The owner is precisely the audience likely to enlarge text. A primary field action should remain visible, and increasing text size should make job identification easier.

**Fix:** Let bottom actions wrap or stack when their labels no longer fit, with adequate content clearance. Use scalable type consistently for names/headings. Verify the full upload and viewer flows at enlarged text settings. This was a browser font-size test, not a claim about every OS text-size setting.

### L2 — High: phone viewer information overlaps the photo and becomes hard to read

**Where / evidence:** Photo viewer, 390×844: [viewer-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/viewer-phone.png); another photo: [deep-link-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/deep-link-phone.png). Desktop reference, 1440×1000: [viewer-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/viewer-desktop.png).

**Observed:** The job name, date, uploader, and file details sit directly over the image. Gray text disappears into the bright wood/photo detail. Five action buttons and a retention paragraph occupy much of the lower screen. There is no separate information toggle in the inspected phone viewer.

**Why it matters:** Users lose both a clear view of the photo and reliable identification of the photo they are acting on.

**Fix:** Keep the image unobscured by default. Put metadata/actions in an opaque, labeled information sheet, with the job name and a clear route to details available from the viewer. Keep the retention explanation beside the trash action/review rather than covering every photo.

### L3 — Medium: everyday controls are too small for this audience

**Where / evidence:** Project, 390×844: [project-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/project-phone.png); viewer, 390×844: [viewer-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/viewer-phone.png); login: [login-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/login-phone.png).

**Observed:** Live geometry measured project filter chips at 30px high, grouping buttons at 28px, Rename at 16px, and viewer action buttons at 34px. Filter and viewer action labels are 12px. Project counts and dates are similarly small and muted. The 46px capture/upload buttons show that the app already has a better-sized pattern.

**Why it matters:** Small labels and closely stacked targets require precision from older users and people working from phones on site.

**Fix:** Use at least 44px touch targets, 16px form/body text, and a deliberate readable secondary-text size. Keep density in the image grid rather than shrinking controls. These measurements identify usability issues; this is not a complete WCAG conformance assessment.

### L4 — Medium: truncated job names make the list hard to recognize

**Where / evidence:** Home/list at 390×844: [home-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/home-phone.png); desktop navigation at 1440×1000: [home-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/home-desktop.png). The full project title is visible after opening: [empty-project-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/empty-project-phone.png).

**Observed:** Long job names are shortened after the same leading words, while the job number and empty count keep their space. On desktop the narrow sidebar truncates many names.

**Why it matters:** A folder-minded user may remember a location or job name rather than its number. Opening rows to discover their identity makes browsing laborious.

**Fix:** Allow two-line names in the job overview and a wider/resizable desktop job list. Provide the full name on keyboard focus and tap wherever truncation remains. Keep the successful wrapped project heading.

### L5 — Medium: the desktop chrome competes with the photos

**Where / evidence:** Library/project, 1440×1000: [home-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/home-desktop.png).

**Observed:** The vivid red Sign out button is the strongest header accent. Trash, Take Photos, Upload, Receipts, and Sign out share the main browsing header, while the current project heading is only modestly stronger than navigation.

**Why it matters:** The page emphasizes account exit and an adjacent product before it establishes “you are looking at this job”.

**Fix:** Move account actions into a quiet account menu, make the current location clearer, and emphasize one relevant capture/upload action. Retain the restrained neutral background and compact global search.

### L6 — Low: small forms are housed in unnecessarily tall dialogs

**Where / evidence:** Desktop edit and upload, 1440×1000: [edit-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/edit-desktop.png), [upload-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/upload-desktop.png); phone upload, 390×844: [upload-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/upload-phone.png).

**Observed:** Large blank areas separate a handful of fields from the action. The desktop edit dialog is roughly 520px tall for two fields and one link; upload is taller still.

**Why it matters:** The forms feel heavier than the tasks and cover far more of the library than necessary.

**Fix:** Size desktop dialogs to their content. On phones, use a compact sheet with a visible header dismissal and a footer that stays reachable when the keyboard opens. Keyboard-open geometry was not tested here.

## Sense

### S1 — High: the library does not answer “where did my folder go?”

**Where / evidence:** Phone home, 390×844: [home-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/home-phone.png); desktop landing, 1440×1000: [home-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/home-desktop.png); project grouped by sheet: [group-sheet-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/group-sheet-phone.png).

**Observed:** Phone home is a list of jobs; desktop lands directly in the first job. Neither inspected entry surface offers a visible folder/import destination. A project contains Date/Sheet grouping, with all 40 inspected items under “No sheet” when grouped by sheet. Numbers such as “518” and “1740” appear beside counts without labels.

**Why it matters:** Someone arriving from Mylio cannot tell how their old organization maps to this library, or whether an unfamiliar number describes a folder, customer, or something else.

**Fix:** In the planned navigation redesign, establish one consistent library overview and explicit location breadcrumbs. Explain how imported folders map to jobs and preserve/show source-folder context where supported. Label or remove the unexplained numbers. Do not imply a preserved hierarchy unless the import actually preserves it.

### S2 — High: move/trash review reads like a batch-processing console

**Where / evidence:** Move review, 390×844 and 1440×1000: [move-review-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/move-review-phone.png), [move-review-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/move-review-desktop.png); trash review, 1440×1000: [trash-review-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/trash-review-desktop.png); trash list, 390×844: [trash-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/trash-phone.png).

**Observed:** A single-photo task introduces “exact targets”, “draft”, “pending”, “expected job”, “concurrent changes”, and “Refresh results”. Confirmation controls precede the small photo card. Trash adds “MCP restore handoff” without a user-facing explanation.

**Why it matters:** The user must interpret system mechanics while deciding whether the right photo will move or disappear. “Pending” is especially ambiguous before anything has been confirmed.

**Fix:** Lead with the photo and a plain “Move this photo from [job] to [job]” or “Move this photo to trash?” summary. Put the verb and consequence together, then “Move photo” / “Cancel”. Keep recovery timing visible. Disclose conflict/batch details only when relevant, using plain recovery instructions. Replace the MCP sentence with the actual contact/action a person can take.

### S3 — Medium: phone editing/upload has no obvious “leave without saving”

**Where / evidence:** 390×844: [edit-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/edit-phone.png), [upload-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/upload-phone.png). Desktop close affordance: [edit-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/edit-desktop.png).

**Observed:** Mobile sheets show a drag handle and Save or Upload, but no visible Cancel/Close control. Their accessibility snapshots also exposed no close button. Escape dismissed the inspected sheets; desktop supplies an X.

**Why it matters:** A non-technical user may feel forced to commit or fear that dismissing the sheet will save. A drag handle alone is not a clear answer.

**Fix:** Add a visible Cancel or labeled Close control in the mobile sheet header and maintain a predictable return to the photo/job. If fields are changed, explain discard behavior. No dirty-form or discard flow was exercised.

### S4 — Medium: “Move” is hidden under “Edit tags”

**Where / evidence:** Viewer and editor, 1440×1000: [viewer-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/viewer-desktop.png), [edit-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/edit-desktop.png); phone editor: [edit-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/edit-phone.png).

**Observed:** The viewer’s action is “Edit tags”; opening it reveals sheet editing and “Review move to another job”.

**Why it matters:** Moving a photo to the correct job is a different task from editing tags. Users will not reliably discover it there.

**Fix:** Expose a clearly named “Move to another job” action in the viewer’s actions menu. Name the metadata form “Edit details” and keep moving outside it.

### S5 — Medium: search changes meaning before and after submission

**Where / evidence:** Typed person search on phone, 390×844: [search-person-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/search-person-phone.png); submitted results: [search-results-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/search-results-phone.png); desktop submitted results, 1440×1000: [search-person-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/search-person-desktop.png).

**Observed:** Typing a known uploader’s name produces “No jobs match”, even though pressing Enter returns 40 photos. Phone exposes a small “Search all photos” link; desktop submission shows “No jobs match” in the sidebar beside successful photo results.

**Why it matters:** The placeholder promises jobs, people, or tags. A user can reasonably interpret the first empty message as a failed search and stop.

**Fix:** Present job suggestions and photo search as explicitly separate result sections, or submit a unified search consistently. Make the search action obvious and give the result page a clear title/count. Avoid a large “no matches” area next to successful results.

### S6 — Medium: filters and metadata require knowledge the screen never supplies

**Where / evidence:** Sheet filter, 390×844 and 1440×1000: [sheet-filter-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/sheet-filter-phone.png), [sheet-filter-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/sheet-filter-desktop.png); upload: [upload-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/upload-phone.png); grouped view: [group-sheet-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/group-sheet-desktop.png).

**Observed:** “Sheet” appears as both a filter and grouping choice, without a “Group by” label. Empty Sheet/Tags menus offer only “None yet”. Upload asks for an optional sheet number and tags with no explanation of how they help.

**Why it matters:** Users must guess whether “Sheet” means a drawing sheet, spreadsheet, album, or replacement folder. Empty menus look unfinished.

**Fix:** Label grouping explicitly. If “Sheet” means a drawing reference, say “Drawing sheet” with an example; otherwise use the actual user term. Explain empty metadata states in one sentence and disclose optional organization fields beneath the job destination.

### S7 — Medium: import starts with technical details instead of reassuring folder guidance

**Where / evidence:** Import, 390×844 and 1440×1000: [migrate-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/migrate-phone.png), [migrate-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/migrate-desktop.png).

**Observed:** Before a folder is selected, the page shows “Foreground migration”, a draft status, XMP counts, sidecar exclusions, committed/pending-work language, an empty inventory, and recent batches identified by short IDs.

**Why it matters:** The owner’s immediate questions—what to select, where the photos will appear, and what happens to their existing folders—are diluted by implementation vocabulary.

**Fix:** Lead with “Import folders” and one focal “Choose folder” action. Explain destination mapping and whether originals/folder structure are changed using verified product behavior. Keep “leave this page open” and resume guidance visible at the appropriate step. Move XMP/exclusion details behind an advanced explanation and name saved imports by source/job and date.

### S8 — Medium: empty projects retain irrelevant controls and a distant next step

**Where / evidence:** Empty long-name job, 390×844 and 1440×1000: [empty-project-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/empty-project-phone.png), [empty-project-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/empty-project-desktop.png).

**Observed:** Four filters and grouping controls remain above “No photos yet — upload the first one.” The message itself has no action. On desktop the actual Upload button is in the far header.

**Why it matters:** This is a frequent visible state in the supplied library. A new user sees several controls that cannot help, and may read emptiness as a failed import.

**Fix:** Use a clear empty-state explanation with an adjacent “Upload photos to this job” action. Remove irrelevant filters until there are photos. Where known, distinguish a new empty job from an import that has not completed.

### S9 — Low: sign-in lacks a recovery cue and product identity

**Where / evidence:** Login and code entry, 390×844: [login-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/login-phone.png), [code-phone.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/code-phone.png); desktop code entry: [code-desktop.png](.artifacts/photo-albums/reviews/2026-09-20-baseline/code-desktop.png).

**Observed:** The logo and generic phone sign-in copy do not say Photos; the browser title is DWS Receipts. Code entry has Change Number but no visible resend/delivery-help route in the inspected state.

**Why it matters:** A person following a photo-library link has less reassurance they are in the right place, and no clear next step if SMS delivery fails.

**Fix:** Identify “DWS Photos” on sign-in and in the title. Add a timed “Resend code” / delivery-help path. Delivery failure was not induced, so this is a missing visible recovery affordance, not a claim that SMS fails.

## Five changes with the greatest payoff

1. **Make phone viewing and enlarged text reliable.** Separate photo information from the image, keep upload actions onscreen, and enlarge everyday touch targets. Addresses L1–L3.
2. **Make the library’s location and folder mapping obvious.** Use a consistent overview, clear job names/breadcrumbs, and explicit import/source-folder context. Addresses S1 and L4.
3. **Simplify edit, move, and trash around one understandable decision.** Show the photo before confirmation, expose Move directly, and provide visible Cancel. Addresses S2–S4 and L6.
4. **Align search and metadata language with what users expect.** Distinguish job suggestions from photo results; explain drawing sheets and hide optional complexity. Addresses S5–S6.
5. **Clean up the supporting screens and hierarchy.** Quiet account chrome, improve readable type, give empty projects a direct action, and make import a guided folder task. Addresses L5 and S7–S9.

## Keep

- **The dark neutral palette and compact photo grid.** Real images dominate; gutters are consistent and the square crops scan well. Date grouping gives the collection useful structure.
- **The desktop viewer’s generous image area.** Next/previous, count, and thumbnail strip support comparison. ArrowRight changed the photo, Tab reached a named Zoom control, and Escape returned to the grid.
- **Phone capture/upload access.** The bottom actions are easy to find at normal text size. Preserve their availability while fixing scaling and content overlap.
- **The selected-job default in upload.** It saves effort and makes the destination visible before uploading.
- **Explicit recoverability.** The 30-day rule and a restore deadline are useful. Keep the facts while simplifying surrounding language.
- **Long titles wrapping on the project page.** The inspected long job name remained readable at 390px and 320px.
- **Photo links and submitted search URLs.** A fresh desktop photo URL opened the intended viewer; submitted search retained its query in the URL.
